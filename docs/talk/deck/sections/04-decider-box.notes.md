## Speaker notes per slide

**D1. System 1 answers, System 2 writes.** A decision model is a classifier you can talk to: options and rubrics come in the request, a probability comes out, one forward pass, no tokens. Three primitives: noul (P(true)), choice (a probability per option), score (an ordered level). The confidence is derived from the distribution, not predicted, and on unseen short tasks 0.9+ is right about 95% of the time. For the car this buys three things: about a hundred milliseconds a question on a desktop GPU (a few hundred on the booth Mac with an image), a number per class that the rover can speak as a reason, and a clean fail-closed rule in code (late or malformed means Deny). The bars are real: the booth model on a frame with a person at desk distance said person 0.95 on the yes/no and put 0.07 on "clear". Say once that "System 2" is your shorthand; the maintainers say "system one models" and "reasoning models".

**D2. JevBench.** Name the board correctly: JevBench by Benchmark Heaven, independent of TypeSafe AI whose Jev model it also scores. Four axes: accuracy, calibration, latency, cost. The field went from 52 systems to 89 in a few weeks and stood at 181 ranked systems when the board was fetched on 17 Oct. Strands Decider's launch rank: 50th overall, 3rd in the 2B class, 1st among strictly-2B models. The top of the open-weights board today is Gemma 4 everywhere: a Gemma-4-31B LoRA leads at 81.7, and nine of the top fourteen sit on a Gemma 4 base. Say Gemma, not Gemini.

**D3. Five sizes, one mechanism.** Same pointer head on five torsos: the 2B on Qwen3.5-2B-Base and four Gemma 4 sizes; 180 to 207 of 231 public tasks, Brier 0.280 down to 0.162. One endpoint, three question types, and many questions over one state are nearly free because the state is encoded once. The booth runs the 2B with `--vision` on the Mac: 141 ms for the envelope yes/no, 349 ms for the frame choice, 490 ms for both, 5.4 GB, healthy ten seconds after start. The Gemma 4 E2B saw every drawn frame correctly but costs 1.1 s per hop and crashed MPS twice: recorded cuts only.

**D4. Giving the decider eyes.** Qwen3.5-2B-Base ships with a vision tower that upstream threw away at load. PR #10 keeps it, maps the published text adapter onto the same decoder inside the multimodal model, and puts the image inside the state. No retraining, and on NaturalBench and POPE it matches an image-trained 2B while being better calibrated. That is what `--vision` is, and it is why the car can ask "is a person in this frame?" at all. PR #67 does the same for images and audio on the Gemma 4 E2B/E4B. The leaderboard line: a 4B candidate scored an estimated number one on the text board under a local proxy scorer; unofficial, and the official run is pending, so say exactly that. decider-lab is the harness all of this ran on, open since 6 Oct: one YAML, any machine, paired confidence intervals so a 0.01 delta is called noise.

**D5. One request per hop.** Walk the loop: the agent proposes a hop; the gate builds a state (command, envelope, and the deterministic check computed in code) and attaches one 480x360 frame; the decider encodes once and returns a typed answer per question; the decision table denies when the person yes/no is at or above 0.50 or the five-way says unclear at or above 0.65 (a weak unclear proceeds and says so), Guides on an obstacle, Confirms on an envelope violation; the command gate publishes. Two lessons measured on 14 Oct: keep the numbers in code (140/140 with the check vs 110/140 without), and let the yes/no only add Denies (it fixed the explanation on 10 of 11 person frames for about 95 ms). The decider explains; physics stops the car.

**D6. Strands Box.** Box is a sandbox engine for the agent process: OS isolation for the filesystem grants in box.toml, and a Dogwood policy (Cedar syntax) for everything the engine sees (shell, Python through Monty, egress, MCP). Default deny, forbid beats permit, decisions made outside the agent, every one logged. The egress gateway swaps a placeholder for the real credential on the way out, so a prompt-injected agent cannot leak a key it never held. Temporal rules use the shared event history. In this stack it is optional and lives on the laptop around the race-engineer agent that has shell access to the car; 0.1.x is macOS on Apple silicon only, and it is never on the car.

**D7. Synthesis.** Four layers, four kinds of guarantee: the LLM proposes (intent), the decider explains (a calibrated number and a sentence), the command gate allow-lists, physics stops. Box confines the process that could reach the car's shell. End on the safety line.
