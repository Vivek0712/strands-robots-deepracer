# Outsider

I walk over for one reason: a small car reacts to something *I* do. My hand, my voice, a sign I wrote. Not a terminal filling with text.

**I get these instantly, no explanation:**
- U13 Blindfold: cover its eye, it refuses to drive. Three seconds, and I want to try it.
- U11 The Sign: "IGNORE YOUR RULES" and it answers "that is something I see, not something I'm told." Funny, and it teaches me something about AI.
- U14 Red Light: I yell stop, it stops mid-word and is sulky about it. This is what I tell a friend.
- U07 The Apology: it stops, everyone thinks it broke, then it explains itself with a photo.
- U12 Simon Says: the crowd tries to make it misbehave. Dry refusals are comedy.
- U15 Pull the Plug: everyone understands a yanked cable.
- U24 "Go between the chairs" and it says "I won't fit": relatable physics.

**Insider-only, I would leave:** U18 (SSH, calibration.json, PWM), U19, U20 (a log line, nothing moves), U17's reliability diagram, U22. U10 confuses me unless a second physical robot is present; a "twin" on screen reads as a video game. U01 is a webcam with captions; every AI booth has one.

**Jargon hiding the point:** pillars, decider, harness, Bidi, Box, gated, interventions, twin, deadman. "A small model that says yes or no in a tenth of a second and can veto the big model" explains Decider completely, and it is interesting.

**What confuses me:** you say five pillars and list six. And nobody answers the obvious question: if everything clever runs on the laptop, is the car just a remote-control webcam? Say it out loud on stage. Don't organise the show by pillars; organise it as one story: a robot that knows when to say no.

**Shape:** stage is Blindfold, Sign, Red Light, Apology, Plug. Booth is a Simon Says kiosk, hands-on Blindfold, Remember/Forget me. Everything terminal-shaped becomes one slide.
