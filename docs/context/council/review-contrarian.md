# Contrarian peer review

**(1) Strongest: C.** It is the only response that prices the plan against the calendar: three weeks to make the car a strands-robots peer, one week of demo. Its spine (deadman, hop driver, frame, decider gate, audit, screen contract) makes eight beats near-free and cuts the rest on build cost, not taste. It names Monday's first action. A says the same more elegantly but without a schedule; D and E agree on the story but hand-wave the build.

**(2) Biggest blind spot: B.** It answers "what do I ship after the conference," not "what survives the stage." Two physical bodies, a workshop series and a dataset are a quarter of work for a solo builder with four weeks. Worse, "the booth is a labelling farm" collides with the consent non-negotiable: attendee frames are PII, and B treats them as inventory.

**(3) All five missed the stop-latency budget.** Everyone leans on "a person steps in, it stops," yet the perception path is MJPEG to laptop to decider to rosbridge to motor, likely 300-700 ms, so 10-20 cm of travel at 0.3 m/s. The deadman covers link loss, not perception lag. Measure it in week two; if it exceeds the pen margin, the pen lip and hop cap are the real guarantee, and the stage script should say so.
